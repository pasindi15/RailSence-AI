RAILSENSE ROLLING STOCK MAINTENANCE MANUAL
Component: Bogie
Document Reference: RSM-BOGIE-2.3
Revision: 4.0 | Effective Date: 2025-01-01

================================================================================
SECTION 1 — OVERVIEW
================================================================================

The bogie is the wheeled undercarriage assembly that supports the car body,
guides the rolling stock along the track, and absorbs dynamic forces from the
rail. Each bogie consists of a bogie frame (BG-FRAME-1100), two wheelsets
(BG-WHEEL-2200), primary suspension springs (BG-SPRING-3300), secondary air
suspension bags (BG-AIRBAG-4400), axle boxes (BG-AXLEBOX-5500), yaw dampers
(BG-DAMPER-6600), and lateral bump stops (BG-STOP-7700).

Bogie integrity is directly linked to ride quality, wheel-rail interaction, and
the safe operation of the braking and traction systems. Defective bogies are a
leading cause of derailment incidents.

================================================================================
SECTION 2 — INSPECTION INTERVALS
================================================================================

2.1 Routine Bogie Inspection
Full bogie inspection every 90 days or 20,000 km, whichever is first.
The bogie must be removed from under the car body for a full inspection.

2.2 Wheel Profile Check
Wheel profile measurement using a wheel profile gauge every 30 days.
Check flange height, flange thickness, and tread wear.

2.3 Axle Box Inspection
Inspect axle box oil level and bearing condition every 90 days.

2.4 Post-Derailment or High-Impact Inspection
After any derailment, buffer impact, or abnormal vibration report, a full
bogie inspection is mandatory before return to service.

================================================================================
SECTION 3 — INSPECTION PROCEDURES
================================================================================

3.1 Vibration Monitoring
Bogie vibration is measured on the bogie frame using an accelerometer.
Vibration can originate from wheel flats, worn bearings, or suspension failure.
- Normal: 0 to 3 mm/s² — serviceable.
- Advisory (AMBER): 3 to 5 mm/s² — inspect wheelset profiles and axle box
  bearings. Check primary suspension springs for fatigue or breakage.
- Critical (RED): above 5 mm/s² — remove from service immediately. Likely
  causes: wheel flat (>2 mm depth), failed axle box bearing (BG-BRG-9900),
  broken suspension spring, or loose bogie frame bolt.
  Do not operate above 5 mm/s² — risk of rail damage and derailment.

3.2 Temperature Monitoring (Axle Box)
Axle box temperature is measured using a handheld IR thermometer or
trackside hot axle box detector reading.
- Normal: ambient +20°C to ambient +40°C.
- Advisory (AMBER): ambient +40°C to ambient +55°C — inspect bearing
  lubrication. Replenish oil to MAX line.
- Critical (RED): above 95°C absolute, or ambient +55°C or more — indicates
  bearing failure or inadequate lubrication. Remove from service immediately.
  A hot axle box above 95°C risks bearing seizure and axle fracture.

3.3 Oil Pressure / Lubrication Check
Axle box oil level must be checked using the sight glass on each axle box.
- Normal: oil level between MIN and MAX marks.
- Low oil (AMBER): below MIN — top up with approved gear oil grade G-90.
- Contaminated oil: milky appearance indicates water ingress. Drain and
  replace with fresh oil. Inspect axle box seals (BG-SEAL-8800).
Oil pressure in the forced lubrication circuit (if fitted):
- Normal: 1.5 to 6.0 bar.
- Low pressure (RED): below 1.5 bar — inspect pump and oil lines.
- Over-pressure (RED): above 6.0 bar — inspect pressure relief valve.

3.4 Wheel Profile Inspection (BG-WHEEL-2200)
Measure flange height and thickness with a calibrated wheel profile gauge.
- Flange height maximum: 36 mm. Condemn wheel if above 36 mm.
- Flange thickness minimum: 22 mm. Condemn wheel if below 22 mm.
- Tread hollow: maximum 5 mm concavity. Beyond 5 mm — condemn and regrind.
- Wheel flat: visible flat spot — measure depth. Above 2 mm depth: mandatory
  immediate reprofiling or wheel replacement.

3.5 Primary Suspension Spring Inspection (BG-SPRING-3300)
- Check for cracked or broken coils (visual inspection with torch).
- Measure free height — compare against baseline in Table 5.1.
- A broken primary spring results in abnormal car body tilt and elevated
  vibration. Replace immediately if a broken spring is found.

3.6 Secondary Air Suspension (BG-AIRBAG-4400)
- Check air bag pressure — normal range 5.0 to 7.0 bar.
- Inspect rubber bellows for cracks, cuts, or abrasion damage.
- A deflated air bag lowers the car body on one side, causing door misalignment
  and passenger discomfort. Replace defective bag within 48 hours.

================================================================================
SECTION 4 — FAULT DIAGNOSIS
================================================================================

4.1 Symptom: Vibration Above 5 mm/s²
Cause 1: Wheel flat spot (BG-WHEEL-2200 flat depth > 2 mm).
Action: Reprogram reprofiling at the next wheel lathe opportunity.
Cause 2: Failed axle box bearing (BG-BRG-9900).
Action: Replace bearing in workshop. Inspect shaft for scoring.
Cause 3: Broken primary suspension spring (BG-SPRING-3300).
Action: Replace spring pair (always replace in pairs per axle).

4.2 Symptom: High Axle Box Temperature (above 95°C)
Cause: Bearing lubrication failure or bearing fatigue.
Action: Remove from service. Drain and inspect axle box.
Replace bearing BG-BRG-9900 and seals BG-SEAL-8800.

4.3 Symptom: Car Body Tilt (uneven ride height)
Cause: Deflated secondary air bag (BG-AIRBAG-4400).
Action: Check air bag pressure. Replace defective bag.

4.4 Symptom: Oil Pressure Below 1.5 Bar
Cause: Lubrication pump failure or blocked oil line.
Action: Inspect pump and lines. Replace pump if output below specification.

================================================================================
SECTION 5 — SERVICE PROCEDURES
================================================================================

5.1 Bogie Removal
1. Raise car body on hydraulic jacks — use designated jack points only.
2. Roll out bogie on maintenance track.
3. Secure bogie frame on bogie overhaul stand.

5.2 Axle Box Bearing Replacement (BG-BRG-9900)
1. Drain axle box oil.
2. Remove axle box end cover (6x M12 bolts, 95 Nm).
3. Press out bearing using hydraulic bearing press BP-660.
4. Clean housing. Inspect for scoring.
5. Press in new bearing (heat to 80°C). Reassemble.
6. Fill with approved gear oil G-90 to MAX line.

5.3 Wheel Reprofiling
Reprofiling must be performed on a CNC wheel lathe. Target profile as per
Table 5.2 (worn rail profile). Minimum wheel diameter after reprofiling: 830 mm.
Condemn wheel if diameter falls below 830 mm.

================================================================================
SECTION 7 — SAFETY WARNINGS
================================================================================

WARNING: Vibration above 5 mm/s² on the bogie is a mandatory withdrawal condition.
WARNING: Hot axle box above 95°C risks bearing seizure — remove from service.
WARNING: Never operate a vehicle with a known wheel flat above 2 mm depth.
WARNING: Always use designated jack points when raising a car body.
WARNING: Broken suspension springs must be replaced in pairs.
