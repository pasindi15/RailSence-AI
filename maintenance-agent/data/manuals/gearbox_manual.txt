RAILSENSE ROLLING STOCK MAINTENANCE MANUAL
Component: Gearbox
Document Reference: RSM-GRBX-7.4
Revision: 2.7 | Effective Date: 2025-01-01

================================================================================
SECTION 1 — OVERVIEW
================================================================================

The traction gearbox transmits torque from the traction motor to the axle,
providing the gear reduction required to match motor speed to axle rotational
speed. The gearbox assembly (part number GB-MAIN-1100) is a single-stage
helical gear unit housed in a sealed, oil-bath lubricated casing. Key
components include the gear pair (GB-GEAR-2200), input shaft bearing
(GB-BRG-3300), output shaft bearing (GB-BRG-3301), shaft seals (GB-SEAL-4400),
oil level plug (GB-OIL-5500), and the gearbox casing (GB-CASE-6600).

Gearbox failure in service causes immediate loss of traction on the affected
axle and may cause damage to the traction motor and bogie.

================================================================================
SECTION 2 — INSPECTION INTERVALS
================================================================================

2.1 Routine Inspection
Full gearbox inspection every 90 days or 20,000 km, whichever is first.

2.2 Oil Level and Quality Check
Check gearbox oil level and quality every 30 days via the inspection plug.

2.3 Oil Change
Change gearbox oil every 180 days regardless of condition.
Use only approved gear oil grade G-220.

2.4 Post-Impact Inspection
After any buffer impact, derailment, or abnormal noise reported by the driver,
a full gearbox inspection is mandatory before return to service.

================================================================================
SECTION 3 — INSPECTION PROCEDURES
================================================================================

3.1 Operating Temperature
Gearbox oil temperature is measured using a probe at the oil drain plug or
via a permanently mounted temperature sensor (if fitted).
- Normal: 50–80°C under normal traction load.
- Advisory (AMBER): 80–95°C — check oil level and quality. Reduce load.
  Elevated temperature indicates low oil level, degraded oil, or early gear
  wear increasing friction.
- Critical (RED): above 95°C — shut down traction on affected axle immediately.
  Operation above 95°C causes oil breakdown, accelerated bearing wear, and
  risk of gear tooth fracture. Inspect oil for metallic particles.

3.2 Oil Pressure (Forced Lubrication, If Fitted)
Some gearbox variants use a forced oil lubrication pump (GB-PUMP-7700).
- Normal oil pressure: 2.0 to 5.0 bar.
- Low pressure (AMBER): 1.5 to 2.0 bar — top up oil, check for leaks.
- Critical low (RED): below 1.5 bar — stop traction. Gear and bearing seizure
  risk. Inspect oil pump GB-PUMP-7700 and all lubrication lines.
- Over-pressure (RED): above 6.0 bar — inspect pressure relief valve.
For gravity-lubricated gearboxes: oil level must be between MIN and MAX marks.
Below MIN is equivalent to the low-pressure AMBER condition above.

3.3 Vibration Measurement
Measure vibration on the gearbox casing during a controlled loaded run.
- Normal: 0 to 3 mm/s² — serviceable.
- Advisory (AMBER): 3 to 5 mm/s² — inspect input shaft bearing (GB-BRG-3300)
  and gear tooth contact pattern at next oil change.
- Critical (RED): above 5 mm/s² — indicates a failing bearing, a chipped or
  cracked gear tooth (GB-GEAR-2200), or a loose gearbox mounting.
  Remove from service immediately. Continued operation risks gear tooth fracture
  and catastrophic gearbox failure.

3.4 Oil Quality Inspection
Remove the oil drain plug and collect a small oil sample.
- Acceptable: clear to light amber, no particles.
- Advisory (AMBER): dark brown colour — oil has exceeded its service life.
  Change oil immediately with G-220. Inspect for root cause of degradation.
- Critical (RED): milky white — water contamination. Drain and flush system.
  Inspect shaft seals (GB-SEAL-4400) and identify water ingress path.
- Critical (RED): metallic particles or swarf visible — gear or bearing is
  shedding material. Do not return to service. Workshop inspection required.
  Inspect gear pair (GB-GEAR-2200) and both bearings (GB-BRG-3300, GB-BRG-3301).

3.5 External Seal and Leak Inspection (GB-SEAL-4400)
- Inspect around input and output shaft seals for oil weeping or active drips.
- Minor seepage (damp but no drips): monitor and plan seal replacement.
- Active drip (drops visible): replace shaft seal at next maintenance window.
  An oil leak reduces the oil level inside the gearbox, leading to lubrication
  failure and the overtemperature condition described in Section 3.1.

3.6 Gear Backlash Check
Measure gear backlash using a dial indicator on the output shaft.
- Acceptable backlash: 0.15–0.35 mm.
- Excessive backlash above 0.40 mm: gear teeth are worn. Schedule overhaul.

================================================================================
SECTION 4 — FAULT DIAGNOSIS
================================================================================

4.1 Symptom: Gearbox Temperature Above 95°C
Cause 1: Oil level below MIN.
Action: Top up with G-220. Investigate source of oil loss.
Cause 2: Degraded oil (dark brown, high viscosity).
Action: Drain and replace with fresh G-220.
Cause 3: Gear or bearing generating heat from wear.
Action: Collect oil sample. If metallic particles found — workshop inspection.

4.2 Symptom: Vibration Above 5 mm/s²
Cause 1: Chipped or cracked gear tooth (GB-GEAR-2200).
Action: Workshop inspection. Replace gear pair.
Cause 2: Worn input shaft bearing (GB-BRG-3300).
Action: Replace bearing. Inspect shaft for scoring.
Cause 3: Loose gearbox mounting bolt.
Action: Inspect all mounting bolts. Torque to 110 Nm.

4.3 Symptom: Oil Pressure Below 1.5 Bar (Forced Lubrication)
Cause: Failed oil pump (GB-PUMP-7700) or blocked oil gallery.
Action: Replace pump. Flush oil gallery with flushing oil.

4.4 Symptom: Metallic Particles in Oil
Cause: Gear tooth fracture or bearing failure.
Action: Full gearbox disassembly in workshop. Replace gear pair and bearings.

4.5 Symptom: Oil Leak at Shaft Seal
Cause: Worn shaft seal (GB-SEAL-4400).
Action: Replace shaft seal. Clean housing mating face before fitting new seal.

================================================================================
SECTION 5 — SERVICE PROCEDURES
================================================================================

5.1 Oil Change
1. Warm gearbox by running for 10 minutes.
2. Remove drain plug and drain fully into a catch tray.
3. If last change was over 360 days ago, flush with flushing oil G-FLUSH.
4. Replace drain plug washer.
5. Fill via filler plug to MAX line (approximately 2.8 litres for standard unit).
6. Run for 5 minutes. Recheck level.

5.2 Shaft Seal Replacement (GB-SEAL-4400)
1. Remove gearbox from bogie (bogie workshop required).
2. Drain oil completely.
3. Remove shaft end cover and circlip.
4. Press out old seal using seal extractor ST-180.
5. Clean shaft and housing bore.
6. Press in new seal — apply thin oil film to seal lips before fitting.
7. Reassemble and refill with G-220.

5.3 Gear Pair Replacement (GB-GEAR-2200)
Full workshop disassembly. Gear pairs must be replaced as a matched set.
After reassembly, verify backlash (Section 3.6) and run-in for 200 km at
50% load before returning to full-load service.

================================================================================
SECTION 7 — SAFETY WARNINGS
================================================================================

WARNING: Gearbox temperature above 95°C is an immediate traction shutdown condition.
WARNING: Vibration above 5 mm/s² indicates gear or bearing failure — do not run.
WARNING: Metallic particles in oil mean internal failure — do not return to service.
WARNING: Never mix oil grades — seal and bearing degradation will result.
WARNING: Oil pressure below 1.5 bar (forced lubrication) means bearing seizure risk.
