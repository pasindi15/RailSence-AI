RAILSENSE ROLLING STOCK MAINTENANCE MANUAL
Component: Pantograph
Document Reference: RSM-PANTO-3.8
Revision: 3.3 | Effective Date: 2025-01-01

================================================================================
SECTION 1 — OVERVIEW
================================================================================

The pantograph is the current-collection device mounted on the roof of electric
multiple units (EMUs) and electric locomotives. It maintains sliding contact
with the overhead contact wire (OCW) to supply traction power to the vehicle.
The pantograph assembly (part number PG-MAIN-1100) includes the collector head
(PG-HEAD-2200), carbon contact strips (PG-STRIP-3300), upper and lower arms
(PG-ARM-4400), air cylinder and pneumatic raising mechanism (PG-CYL-5500),
contact-force spring (PG-SPRING-6600), and base insulator (PG-INS-7700).

Pantograph faults cause power collection failures and can damage overhead
infrastructure, leading to widespread service disruption.

================================================================================
SECTION 2 — INSPECTION INTERVALS
================================================================================

2.1 Routine Inspection
Full pantograph inspection every 90 days or 15,000 km, whichever is first.
Inspect from the roof or on a pantograph inspection pit.

2.2 Carbon Strip Inspection
Inspect carbon contact strips (PG-STRIP-3300) every 30 days.
Measure strip thickness and inspect for cracks or uneven wear.

2.3 Contact Force Measurement
Measure static contact force every 90 days using a calibrated pull gauge.

2.4 Post-Dewirement Inspection
After any unplanned pantograph lowering, contact wire strike, or dewirement,
a full inspection is mandatory before re-raising and returning to service.

================================================================================
SECTION 3 — INSPECTION PROCEDURES
================================================================================

3.1 Carbon Contact Strip Inspection (PG-STRIP-3300)
The carbon strips are the primary wear element. They must be inspected and
replaced to prevent metal-on-metal contact with the overhead wire, which
causes wire and pantograph damage.
- Normal strip thickness: 12–22 mm.
- Advisory (AMBER): 8–12 mm — plan replacement at next maintenance window.
- Critical (RED): below 8 mm — replace before raising pantograph.
Inspect for longitudinal cracks along the strip length. A cracked strip can
snag the overhead wire. Replace any strip showing visible cracks regardless
of thickness.

3.2 Contact Force Check
Pantograph contact force must be within specification to ensure reliable
current collection without bouncing (low force) or wire damage (high force).
- Normal contact force: 60–90 N at 25 km/h to 160 km/h operating speed.
- Low force (AMBER): below 60 N — inspect contact spring (PG-SPRING-6600).
  Low contact force causes current interruptions and radio-frequency interference.
- High force (RED): above 120 N — inspect for foreign object or jammed
  raising mechanism. High force damages the overhead contact wire.
Measure static contact force with pantograph raised at rest (no movement).
Normal static force: 70 ± 10 N.

3.3 Temperature Monitoring
Measure pantograph collector head temperature using an IR thermometer after
a high-current run (e.g., after ascending a gradient).
- Normal: 40–80°C.
- Advisory (AMBER): 80–95°C — inspect carbon strip contact surface and
  verify uniform contact across the full strip width.
- Critical (RED): above 95°C — indicates a high-resistance contact joint
  (arc burn on strip or collector head), inadequate contact force, or badly
  worn strip. Remove pantograph from service. Inspect head assembly for arc
  damage. Overheating at the contact point can weld the strip to the wire
  or cause an electrical fire.

3.4 Vibration Level
Measure vibration on the pantograph base frame during low-speed run (40 km/h).
- Normal: 0 to 3 mm/s².
- Advisory (AMBER): 3 to 5 mm/s² — inspect arm pivot bearings and dampers.
- Critical (RED): above 5 mm/s² — indicates worn pivot bearings, loose arm
  assembly, or broken damper (PG-DAMP-8800). Do not raise pantograph above
  slow-speed limit until fault is rectified.

3.5 Air Cylinder and Pneumatic Circuit (PG-CYL-5500)
- Raising pressure: 3.5 to 5.5 bar applied to raise the pantograph.
- Check for leaks on air supply hose and cylinder rod seal.
- Oil pressure / air pressure normal range: 1.5 to 6.0 bar in supply circuit.
- Below 1.5 bar: pantograph may not raise reliably. Inspect air supply valve.
- Above 6.0 bar: excessive raising force — inspect pressure regulator.

3.6 Insulator Inspection (PG-INS-7700)
- Inspect insulator for cracks, chips, surface contamination (dust, salt, oil).
- Clean insulator surface with approved insulator cleaner every 90 days.
- A contaminated or cracked insulator can cause a trackable earth fault.
  Replace cracked insulators immediately.

================================================================================
SECTION 4 — FAULT DIAGNOSIS
================================================================================

4.1 Symptom: Pantograph Temperature Above 95°C
Cause 1: Worn carbon strip reducing contact area.
Action: Measure strip thickness. If below 12 mm, replace PG-STRIP-3300.
Cause 2: Low contact force causing arcing.
Action: Check contact spring tension (PG-SPRING-6600). Adjust or replace.
Cause 3: Arc burn on collector head creating high-resistance joint.
Action: Inspect collector head (PG-HEAD-2200). Replace if arc damage found.

4.2 Symptom: Vibration Above 5 mm/s²
Cause: Worn pivot bearings or broken damper.
Action: Replace arm bearings and damper assembly PG-DAMP-8800.

4.3 Symptom: Pantograph Will Not Raise
Cause 1: Insufficient air pressure (below 3.5 bar).
Action: Check air supply. Inspect supply valve and hose.
Cause 2: Jammed raising mechanism.
Action: Inspect arm assembly for foreign object obstruction. Do not force raise.

4.4 Symptom: Cracked Carbon Strip
Cause: Impact with overhead wire anomaly or end-of-life wear.
Action: Replace PG-STRIP-3300 immediately. Inspect for wire damage.

================================================================================
SECTION 5 — SERVICE PROCEDURES
================================================================================

5.1 Carbon Strip Replacement (PG-STRIP-3300)
1. Lower pantograph fully and isolate power supply.
2. Lock out pantograph raising circuit.
3. Remove strip retaining clips (4x per strip).
4. Slide out worn strip and discard.
5. Inspect collector head for arc burns.
6. Insert new strip — ensure correct alignment in receiver channel.
7. Refit retaining clips. Check clip seating.
8. Record strip thickness in maintenance log.

5.2 Contact Spring Replacement (PG-SPRING-6600)
Full pantograph disassembly required. Replace spring and re-measure
static contact force to verify 70 ± 10 N before returning to service.

5.3 Full Pantograph Overhaul (Every 360 Days)
Replace carbon strips, arm bearings, damper, air cylinder seals, and
insulators. Clean all metalwork and reapply anti-corrosion treatment.

================================================================================
SECTION 7 — SAFETY WARNINGS
================================================================================

WARNING: Never raise pantograph without verifying air pressure is 3.5 bar minimum.
WARNING: Temperature above 95°C at collector head is an immediate service withdrawal.
WARNING: Always lock out raising circuit before working on pantograph.
WARNING: Do not operate with carbon strip below 8 mm — overhead wire damage risk.
WARNING: Cracked insulators must be replaced before next raise — earth fault risk.
