# Sri Lanka Railways — Track Maintenance Reference
# Applies to: Permanent Way, All Routes
# Document Reference: SLR-MM-TR-005 | Revision 6

## 1 Rail Types and Standards
Sri Lanka Railways mainline track uses 45 kg/m UIC 54 flat-bottom rail on concrete sleepers with elastic fastenings. Branch and secondary lines use 30 kg/m flat-bottom or bullhead rail on timber sleepers. Rail hardness: minimum 260 HB for head-hardened rail on curves below 400m radius. Standard track gauge is 1676 mm (broad gauge) on mainline and 762 mm (narrow gauge) on the hill country lines between Peradeniya and Badulla. Maximum permitted speed depends on track class: Class A (160 km/h), Class B (120 km/h), Class C (80 km/h), Class D (50 km/h). Track class is determined by condition, not just design speed.

## 2 Sleeper and Tie Inspection
Concrete sleepers must be inspected every 90 days. Condemn sleepers with end cracks greater than 300mm, centre cracks running the full length, broken shoulders, or missing or broken fastening inserts. Timber sleepers must be inspected every 30 days. Timber sleepers showing rot (soft spots when sounded with a hammer), spike pull-out (spike holds less than 2 kN in pull-out test), or through cracks must be replaced immediately. A run of 5 or more defective sleepers in sequence requires an immediate speed restriction to 40 km/h until replacement is complete. Sleeper condition directly affects track geometry and rail fastening security. Annual spike driving or fastener torque checks are required for all timber sleeper sections. New concrete sleepers must not be placed adjacent to cracked sleepers without addressing the cause of failure.

## 3 Ballast Maintenance
Ballast must consist of crushed granite or hard stone angular particles, 19-50mm in size. Minimum ballast depth below sleeper base is 300mm on mainline and 250mm on branch lines. Ballast voids (spaces under sleepers with insufficient support) cause pumping, which accelerates track geometry degradation. Detect ballast voids by the hollow sound when a sleeper is tapped, by visible sleeper movement under load, or by geometry recording car output showing cyclic irregularities. Tamping restores sleeper support by inserting vibrating tines under each sleeper and squeezing ballast inward. After tamping, allow 48 hours for ballast to consolidate before restoring full line speed. Top-ballasting (adding clean ballast) is required when depth falls below minimum. Fouled ballast (contaminated with clay, mud, or fines) must be screened or replaced. Fouled ballast cannot be restored to specification by tamping alone.

## 4 Track Geometry Checks
The six key track geometry parameters are: gauge (1676 ± 6mm on straight track), cross-level (difference in rail head height across the track, maximum 10mm on straight, 5mm on curves), twist (change in cross-level over 2m length, maximum 5mm), alignment (lateral deviation from design centreline, maximum 10mm), longitudinal level (vertical deviation from design profile, maximum 15mm), and curvature (deviation from design radius). Manual geometry checks with a level and gauge must be done every 30 days on all lines. Continuous geometry recording by a track recording car must be done every 6 months on mainline. Any parameter outside the maintenance action limit requires immediate repair. Any parameter outside the intervention limit requires an emergency speed restriction.

## 5 Welded Joint Inspection
Continuously welded rail (CWR) is used on mainlines. Joints between rail lengths are thermite welded to eliminate the expansion gap and reduce impact loading. Inspect welds every 90 days using ultrasonic flaw detection. Transverse defects (cracks perpendicular to the rail running surface) are the most dangerous defect type and require rail replacement when found. Horizontal split head defects (separations between the head and web of the rail) must be repaired within 5 days. Surface defects (head checking, squats, flaking) must be monitored monthly and ground when depth exceeds 0.5mm. If an ultrasonic defect is found at a weld, cut out the weld and replace with a 40-metre rail section to eliminate both the defect and the heat-affected zone. Mark defective rail with yellow paint and apply a 25 km/h speed restriction until replacement is complete. Rail crack or fracture found in service requires immediate possession of the track and repair before any train movement is permitted.

## 6 Drainage and Earthworks
Poor drainage is the primary cause of track geometry degradation. Inspect drainage channels, culverts, and side drains every 30 days. Clear all blockages. Earthwork failures including slips, heaves, and embankment erosion must be reported to the Civil Engineer within 24 hours. A slip or embankment failure adjacent to the track requires immediate possession and speed restriction or closure until the stability of the trackbed is confirmed by a geotechnical assessment. Track sections with ballast void percentage above 20 percent require urgent tamping and drainage investigation. A ballast void percentage above 35 percent requires a speed restriction to 40 km/h and emergency maintenance.
