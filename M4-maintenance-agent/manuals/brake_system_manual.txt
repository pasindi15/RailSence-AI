# Sri Lanka Railways — Brake System Technical Manual
# Applies to: Air Brake and Vacuum Brake Systems, All Rolling Stock
# Document Reference: SLR-MM-BR-002 | Revision 3

## 1 Brake System Overview
Sri Lanka Railways rolling stock uses two types of braking systems. Newer locomotive-hauled stock and multiple units use automatic air brakes operating at 5.0 bar. Older coaching stock uses vacuum brakes operating at 635 mmHg vacuum. This manual covers maintenance procedures for both systems. The automatic air brake is fail-safe: loss of pipe pressure applies the brakes automatically. Brake systems must be inspected every 30 days and tested after any maintenance procedure. Any brake system failure requires immediate withdrawal of the vehicle from service.

## 2 Air Brake Components
The air brake system consists of an air compressor, main reservoir (9.5 bar), brake pipe (5.0 bar), distributor valve, auxiliary reservoir, brake cylinder, and brake blocks or pads. The compressor charges the main reservoir. The driver's brake valve controls brake pipe pressure. Reducing brake pipe pressure below 5.0 bar causes the distributor valve to apply the brakes in proportion. Full service application reduces brake pipe to 3.4 bar. Emergency application exhausts brake pipe to 0 bar. Check all pipe joints and unions for air leaks every 30 days. Air loss greater than 0.3 bar per minute from a fully charged system is unacceptable and must be rectified before service.

## 3 Brake Pad Inspection
Brake pad thickness must be checked every 30 days. Minimum allowable pad thickness is 5mm for disc brakes and 15mm for block brakes. Worn pads cause increased stopping distance and risk of rotor or drum damage. Replace brake pads when thickness reaches 7mm (disc) or 20mm (block) to allow for variation in wear rate. Brake pad wear increases significantly if the asset has experienced brake fade events. Glazed brake shoes must be replaced, not cleaned. New pads require a bedding-in procedure: 5 moderate applications from 60 km/h to 20 km/h before full service. Stopping distance at 60 km/h with air brakes fully applied should not exceed 250 metres. Stopping distance exceeding 320 metres indicates significant brake degradation; withdraw vehicle from service.

## 4 Brake Cylinder Maintenance
Brake cylinders must be inspected for air or fluid leaks every 30 days. A cylinder leaking more than 0.1 bar per minute must be overhauled immediately. The piston seal (O-ring) is the most common failure point and must be replaced during each annual overhaul. Brake cylinder stroke must not exceed the manufacturer's limit (typically 175mm for standard cylinders). Excessive stroke indicates excessive pad wear or cylinder push-rod misalignment. Lubricate the cylinder bore with a light film of brake cylinder oil during overhaul. Do not use petroleum-based grease on air brake piston seals. When testing brake cylinder operation, ensure full release occurs within 3 seconds of releasing the brake.

## 5 Fault Codes and Diagnosis
Soft or spongy brake pedal: air in the hydraulic circuit or partially deflated brake cylinder. Bleed air from the system and retest. Brakes dragging after release: distributor valve not releasing, or sticking brake pads. Inspect distributor valve internals and pad clearance. Excessive stopping distance: worn pads, glazed shoes, air leak, or low reservoir pressure. Check all components systematically. Brake fade during descent: pads overheated. Allow cooling, inspect pad material and condition, replace if glazed. Air compressor not maintaining pressure: worn compressor rings, leaking check valve, or blocked air filter. Overhaul compressor if pressure drops below 7.0 bar under load. Brake pipe pressure unstable: driver's brake valve worn seals. Brake cylinder not releasing fully: bent push-rod or seized clevis pin.

## 6 Emergency Procedures
If air brake system fails in service, use the parking brake to stop the train at the nearest station or safe location. Do not attempt to continue the journey. Radio control room immediately using emergency channel. Apply wheel chocks before any maintenance is performed under a vehicle with a failed brake system. Never release the parking brake until air brakes have been confirmed operational. If brake pipe continuity is lost (e.g. coupling failure), emergency brakes will apply automatically on all vehicles. Conduct a brake continuity test before each journey.

## 7 Vacuum Brake System
Older Sri Lanka Railways coaching stock operates vacuum brakes at 635 mmHg. The vacuum brake pipe runs the full length of the train. The ejector on the locomotive creates vacuum; reducing vacuum (admitting air) applies the brakes. Minimum operating vacuum for full brake release is 600 mmHg. Measure with a vacuum gauge at the locomotive and at the rear of the train; a difference greater than 30 mmHg indicates a pipe leak. The vacuum brake cylinder stroke for full application must be between 130 and 180 mm. Brake block thickness for vacuum-braked stock: minimum 25 mm for cast iron blocks, replace when worn to 30 mm. Inspect all vacuum hose connections (flexible coupling hoses between vehicles) every 30 days; cracked or collapsed hoses reduce brake pipe vacuum and impair braking. Replace any hose showing external cracking or that collapses more than 20 percent of bore diameter when subject to full vacuum.

## 8 Parking Brake and Hand Brake
Parking brakes (hand brakes) must be capable of holding the vehicle on the steepest gradient on the route. For SLR mainline, the maximum gradient is 1 in 44; the parking brake must hold on this gradient with a safety factor of 1.3. Test holding force annually on a calibrated test gradient. Inspect the hand brake chain or screw mechanism for wear and corrosion every 30 days. Lubricate all pivot points with lithium grease. The hand brake must apply fully in no more than 15 turns of the handwheel for screw-type mechanisms. More turns indicate brake rigging wear. A worn hand brake mechanism must be adjusted or repaired before the next service. All vehicles must have functioning parking brakes before leaving a maintenance depot. Tag defective vehicles clearly with a RED OUT OF SERVICE placard attached to the hand brake wheel.

## 9 Brake Testing Procedures
After any brake maintenance, the following tests must be performed before return to service. Air brake continuity test: couple all vehicles, charge the brake pipe to 5.0 bar, close the driver's brake valve isolating cock, and observe pressure drop over 5 minutes; a drop greater than 0.1 bar per minute indicates a pipe or connection leak. Brake application test: apply a full service brake application (reduce brake pipe to 3.4 bar) and verify all brake cylinders apply fully within 4 seconds. Brake release test: restore brake pipe to 5.0 bar and verify all brake cylinders fully release within 5 seconds. Stopping distance test: perform a full service brake application from 60 km/h on level track; stopping distance must not exceed 250 metres. Emergency brake test: perform an emergency application (reduce brake pipe to 0 bar) from 30 km/h; stopping distance must not exceed 75 metres.

## 10 Anti-Skid System (Wheel Slip Protection)
Modern air-braked stock is fitted with an anti-skid (wheel slide protection) system that reduces brake cylinder pressure when wheel lock-up is detected by the axle speed sensors. Axle speed sensors produce a pulse output proportional to wheel speed. Test the anti-skid system by applying full brakes at 30 km/h on low-adhesion track (wet or sanded); the system should cycle brake pressure to prevent wheel lock. Anti-skid module fault codes must be read at each 90-day service with the diagnostic tool. A failed anti-skid system causes wheel flat spots within a short distance of brake application on low-adhesion track; reduce maximum brake application force to 80 percent until the system is repaired.
