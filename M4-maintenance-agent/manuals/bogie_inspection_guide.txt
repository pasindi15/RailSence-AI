# Sri Lanka Railways — Bogie Inspection and Maintenance Guide
# Applies to: All Passenger Coaches, Freight Wagons, and Multiple Units
# Document Reference: SLR-MM-BG-003 | Revision 2

## 1 Bogie Types and Overview
Sri Lanka Railways operates three main bogie types. The Y25 freight bogie is used on general freight wagons. The MD52 passenger bogie is used on coaching stock. The powered bogie with four DC traction motors is used on diesel and electric multiple units. The bogie carries the vehicle body, transmits traction and braking forces, and provides ride quality through its suspension system. A bogie consists of the bogie frame, wheelsets (axle and wheels), axle bearings, primary suspension (coil springs and dampers), secondary suspension (coil springs or air bags), brake rigging, and traction links. Bogies must be inspected visually every 30 days and undergo a full strip-down overhaul every 4 years or 500,000 km.

## 2 Wheel Inspection
Wheel profile must be checked every 30 days using a profile gauge. Minimum wheel flange height is 28mm and minimum wheel diameter above flange is 770mm for standard coaches. A flat spot (wheel flat) greater than 50mm in length must be repaired before the next service. Flat spots cause characteristic thumping vibration at low speed that increases with train speed. Flat spots are caused by wheel lock-up during heavy braking, usually in combination with worn brake pads or a failed anti-skid system. Repair by turning the wheel on a wheel lathe to restore the standard profile. Maximum wheel diameter difference within a wheelset is 1mm. Maximum diameter difference between wheelsets on the same bogie is 5mm. Replace wheels when worn below the minimum diameter or when re-profiling cannot restore the standard profile within the remaining material allowance.

## 3 Axle Bearing Maintenance
Roller bearings are fitted to all axle journals. Bearing temperature should not exceed 60 degrees Celsius under normal service. Bearing temperature above 70 degrees Celsius requires immediate attention; above 80 degrees requires withdrawal from service. Bearing temperature is monitored by hand-held infrared thermometers at every terminal or by a hot axle box detection system on main lines. Bearing failure is indicated by unusual noise (rumbling, grinding, or squealing), high temperature, or excessive axle play. Bearing grease must be renewed every 4 years. Use only lithium complex grease conforming to NLGI Grade 2 for axle bearings. Grease contamination with water or grit reduces service life significantly. After any axle bearing replacement, the bogie must run a 50 km test before returning to full service.

## 4 Suspension System
The primary suspension isolates the bogie frame from track irregularities. Inspect coil springs for cracks and permanent set every 30 days. Replace any spring with a crack or a set (compression under tare weight) greater than 10mm. Hydraulic dampers must be replaced when oil leakage is visible or when damping force is less than 70 percent of specification on a test rig. The secondary suspension carries the vehicle body on the bogie. Air bag secondary suspension must be checked for pressure and leaks every 30 days. Air bag pressure is typically 6.0 to 7.5 bar. A deflated air bag causes the vehicle to lean toward the deflated side and must be repaired before service. Coil spring secondary suspension must be inspected for cracks and permanent set the same as primary springs.

## 5 Vibration Analysis
Vibration level is the primary health indicator for bogie condition. Normal vibration level is below 2.0 on the standard 0-10 scale measured by the onboard sensor. Vibration between 3.0 and 5.0 indicates attention required within 7 days. Vibration above 5.0 indicates significant fault that may require service withdrawal. Vibration analysis helps distinguish fault type: wheel flat produces periodic impulse vibration at a frequency related to wheel circumference and speed, bearing failure produces higher-frequency broad-band vibration increasing with speed, suspension failure produces low-frequency body oscillation not strongly related to speed. When vibration exceeds 5.0, measure wheel profile and bearing temperature before condemning the bogie. Use a 100km test run at reduced speed (60 km/h maximum) to verify vibration reduction after maintenance.

## 6 Replacement Intervals
Wheel tyres: condemn at minimum diameter. Full bearing replacement: every 500,000 km or at strip-down. Coil springs: replace any with cracks or set; otherwise at strip-down. Hydraulic dampers: when leaking or below 70 percent specification. Air bags: when leaking or at 8 years. Brake rigging pins and bushes: at strip-down. Traction links and anti-roll bars: inspect annually, replace if cracked or worn. Bogie frame: inspect by magnetic particle testing at each strip-down; condemn if cracked.
