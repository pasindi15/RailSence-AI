# Sri Lanka Railways — Platform Gate and Passenger Door Maintenance Manual
# Applies to: Platform Screen Doors, Sliding Platform Gates, EMU Passenger Doors
# Document Reference: SLR-MM-PG-008 | Revision 1

## 1 Platform Gate Types and Overview
Sri Lanka Railways operates platform gates on modern urban stations and EMU (Electric Multiple Unit) rolling stock. Platform Screen Doors (PSDs) are full-height or half-height barriers fixed to the platform edge that open and close in synchronisation with train doors. Sliding Platform Gates are simpler mechanically-operated barriers that prevent passenger access to the track area when no train is present. EMU passenger door systems are the sliding or plug doors fitted to the rolling stock. All platform gate systems are safety-critical: a malfunction that allows access to the track without a train present, or that fails to open when a train is present, is a Class 1 safety incident requiring immediate incident report.

## 2 Platform Screen Door Mechanism
Platform screen doors use electric-motor-driven belt or screw drives to slide door panels horizontally. The drive motor is typically a brushless DC motor rated at 150 to 300 W. Motor current during normal operation must not exceed 120 percent of rated current. Motor current above 150 percent of rated current during door travel indicates obstruction or mechanical binding; the door controller triggers a retract and re-attempt sequence. If two re-attempts fail, the door enters fault mode and must be reset manually. Inspect the door runner (upper and lower guide rail) for dirt accumulation, debris, and worn guide pads every 30 days. Clean the runners with a dry cloth and apply a thin film of silicone-based lubricant; never use oil-based lubricants as these attract dust and cause sticking. Door gap sensors (light beam or pressure-sensitive edge) must activate door reversal within 200 milliseconds of obstruction contact. Test the obstacle detection by placing a 30 mm diameter test cylinder in the door path during a close cycle; the door must reverse and re-open. Obstruction detection failure is a critical fault; do not return the door to service until repaired.

## 3 Sliding Platform Gate Mechanism
Sliding platform gates are heavier structures driven by a DC linear actuator or motor-reduction gearbox. Gate travel time from full open to full closed must be between 4 and 8 seconds. Travel time outside this range indicates motor or gearbox wear or binding in the guide rails. Inspect gate wheels or rollers on the lower guide rail every 30 days; replace worn rollers before the guide rail itself becomes damaged. Check the gate lock (locking pin or magnetic lock) engages fully within 1 second of gate reaching the closed position. Magnetic lock holding force must be above 1,500 N; test with a calibrated load cell every 6 months. Gate end-stop buffers must be replaced when compressed more than 50 percent of original thickness. Inspect gate suspension brackets for cracks every 30 days; a cracked bracket risks gate derailment.

## 4 EMU Passenger Door System
EMU passenger doors are sliding or plug-type operated by pneumatic or electro-pneumatic actuators. Pneumatic door systems: air supply pressure must be 6.5 to 7.5 bar at the door cylinder. Measure pressure at the test point on the air supply manifold. Air supply pressure below 6.0 bar causes slow door operation and reduced closing force. Door opening and closing time: 2 to 4 seconds from command to full travel. Slow door operation (greater than 4 seconds) indicates low air pressure, worn seals in the door cylinder, or a bent door panel rubbing on the frame. Door edge protection (rubber edge profile) must be replaced when compressed more than 30 percent of original cross-section; worn edge protection reduces obstacle detection sensitivity. The passenger door interlock must prevent train departure if any door is not fully closed and locked. Test interlock operation at each quarterly service by manually holding one door open and verifying the departure enable output is not given. Door control electronics: the door control unit (DCU) manages individual door operation and reports status to the train management system via MVB or CAN bus. DCU fault codes must be read with the approved diagnostic tool at each 90-day service.

## 5 Door Locking and Interlock System
Platform gate locking mechanisms are safety-critical. For PSDs, the door must be mechanically locked in the closed position; electrical power failure must not unlock the door (fail-safe to locked). For EMU doors, a deadlock is applied when the train is moving above 5 km/h; the deadlock cannot be released by passenger force below 800 N. Inspect deadlock mechanism at each 30-day service; lubricate the locking pawl with PTFE-based dry lubricant. Do not use oil-based lubricants on locking mechanisms. The door closed and locked sensor provides a digital output to the door controller and train management system. Sensor contact resistance must be below 0.5 Ω when the door is locked. Test all door closed and locked sensors using the approved door test mode: each door is individually closed and locked and the sensor output is verified.

## 6 Control System and Communication
PSD control systems communicate with the train via a transponder on the train and a fixed beacon on the platform. Communication must be established within 2 seconds of the train stopping at the correct position. If communication is not established, the PSD system holds all doors closed and alerts the station controller. Train positioning accuracy for PSD operation must be within ± 300 mm of the design stopping point; positioning error greater than ± 500 mm causes door misalignment and must be corrected by train crew re-positioning. The PSD control panel at each station allows the station master to manually open or close all PSDs in an emergency. Test the manual override at each 30-day service. Individual door manual release: each door panel has a manual release handle accessible from the platform side. Test manual release operation at each 90-day service.

## 7 Fault Diagnosis
Door does not open when train arrives: check transponder communication link, check door controller power supply, check PSD motor power supply, check for mechanical binding by attempting manual release. Door does not close fully: inspect door runner for obstruction, check motor current (high current indicates obstruction), test door gap sensor activation, check if door panel is bent or warped. Door opens and immediately reverses: door gap sensor triggering falsely; check for loose wiring at sensor, check for debris on sensor face, test sensor with test cylinder. Door closed sensor not registering: check lock engagement (mechanical), test sensor contact resistance, check sensor wiring. Slow door travel (greater than rated time): check air pressure (pneumatic) or motor current (electric), inspect runners for dirt or damage, lubricate as required. Intermittent door fault codes with no visible cause: check CAN or MVB bus communication between DCU and train management system, check for loose connectors at DCU. Door motor overtemperature: check duty cycle (doors opened and closed too frequently in a short period), check motor cooling vents for blockage, check motor current for sustained overload.

## 8 Safety Checks and Passenger Risk Control
Platform gates must never be left in a position that allows uncontrolled passenger access to the track. If a platform gate or PSD cannot be closed and locked, immediately: close the affected bay manually and secure with the provided hasp and padlock, place physical barriers (crowd control barriers) to prevent passenger access, inform station control and arrange for a temporary station operating procedure until the gate is repaired. Record all gate faults and repairs in the Gate Fault Log (Form PG-1). Recurring faults on the same gate (more than 3 times in 30 days) must be escalated to the Rolling Stock or Infrastructure engineer for root cause investigation. Never allow a train to depart with an EMU door not confirmed locked. If a door lock cannot be confirmed, isolate the affected door using the door isolation switch (seals door closed in locked position) and inform passengers of the locked door location.

## 9 Scheduled Maintenance Intervals
Daily: Visual inspection of all platform gates for damage, debris on runners, and warning lights operational. Test obstruction detection on one door per inspection (rotate through all doors). Monthly: Full functional test of all doors including open, close, lock, and obstacle detection. Clean all door runners and lubricate with approved lubricant. Inspect all door edge protection seals. Test manual release on one door. Check door travel times. Quarterly: Read all DCU fault codes with diagnostic tool. Test door interlock (departure enable with one door held open). Inspect door lock mechanism, lubricate locking pawl. Measure all door closed and locked sensor contact resistances. Inspect gate wheels or rollers for wear. Test magnetic lock holding force. Annual: Full mechanical overhaul of drive mechanism. Replace door edge protection if compressed. Overhaul door cylinder seals (pneumatic). Replace worn guide pads and rollers. Test communication link signal strength (transponder-beacon). Calibrate all door position sensors. Full review of Gate Fault Log with engineering team.
