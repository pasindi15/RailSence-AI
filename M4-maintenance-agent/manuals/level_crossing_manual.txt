# Sri Lanka Railways — Level Crossing Maintenance Manual
# Applies to: Automatic Half-Barrier, Full-Barrier, and Manually-Operated Level Crossings
# Document Reference: SLR-MM-LC-007 | Revision 3

## 1 Level Crossing Types and Overview
Sri Lanka Railways operates three categories of level crossings. Class A crossings are automatic half-barrier (AHB) crossings on mainline routes, activated by approaching train detection. Class B crossings are manually operated gates controlled by a crossing keeper. Class C crossings are unprotected accommodation crossings at low-traffic private roads. This manual covers maintenance of Class A automatic crossings in detail. Automatic crossings must operate safely and reliably because failure to lower barriers before a train arrives creates direct risk of fatal collision. All Class A crossing maintenance must be logged and signed off by a Signal and Telecommunications technician.

## 2 Train Detection System
Automatic crossings use approach track circuits or axle counters to detect approaching trains at a defined distance. For a line speed of 90 km/h, the detection distance is a minimum 1,500 metres. Track circuit approach detection relies on current flowing between rails through the train's axle. Rail joints within the approach track must have bonded rail bonds (copper braided cables bolted to the rail web). Inspect all rail bonds every 30 days; replace any bond showing 50 percent or more stranding broken. Measure rail bond resistance with a Ducter low-resistance ohmmeter; bond resistance above 0.01 Ω requires replacement. Track circuit transmitter output voltage must be within ± 5 percent of nominal. Track circuit receiver relay must pick up (energise) within 500 milliseconds of train axle entering the circuit. Axle counter crossings use inductive wheel detection sensors mounted at the rail head; the sensor air gap must be 5 ± 1 mm. Clean sensor faces monthly with a clean dry cloth; metal shavings from wheel flange wear can bridge the sensor gap and cause false activations.

## 3 Barrier Motor and Drive System
The barrier arm is driven by a single-phase or three-phase AC motor through a reduction gearbox. Motor rated current: 2.5 A (half-barrier), 4.0 A (full-barrier). During normal operation, motor current must not exceed 4.5 A for half-barriers or 6.0 A for full-barriers. High motor current indicates mechanical resistance in the drive mechanism. Inspect the barrier arm pivot bearing, gearbox, and clutch every 30 days. Lubricate the pivot bearing with lithium complex grease NLGI Grade 2 every 30 days. Gearbox oil level must be checked every 90 days; use SAE 90 gear oil. The friction clutch on the barrier output shaft allows the barrier arm to break away without damage if struck by a vehicle. Clutch breakaway torque must be set between 40 and 60 Nm; test with a torque wrench adapter at each annual inspection. After a vehicle strike, inspect the barrier arm, hinge, and clutch before returning to service. The barrier lowering time from receipt of train detection signal must be 7 seconds maximum; measure with a stopwatch during test activation. The barrier must be fully lowered (within 5 degrees of horizontal) before the train can reach the crossing.

## 4 Barrier Arm and Counterweight
Barrier arms are typically GRP (glass reinforced plastic) or aluminium alloy, 3 to 6 metres in length. Inspect arms every 30 days for cracks, delamination (GRP), dents (aluminium), and secure attachment at the pivot end. A cracked barrier arm must be replaced before the next service. Red and white reflective banding on the barrier arm must be maintained; faded or damaged banding must be repainted or the banding tape replaced annually. Red LED warning lamps are fitted at the tip of the barrier arm; replace faulty LEDs within 24 hours of discovery. The counterweight balances the barrier arm so the arm tends to rise on power failure. Counterweight adjustment: the barrier arm should rise slowly under counterweight alone when the motor is disconnected; it must fully raise within 30 seconds under gravity. If the arm does not rise freely, check for pivot bearing seizure, bent arm, or incorrectly sized counterweight.

## 5 Road Warning Lights and Audible Warning
Red flashing road warning lights must activate when the train detection circuit energises, before the barriers begin to lower. The sequence is: detection energised → road lights flash → audible warning activates → barriers begin lowering. All steps must occur within 1 second of detection. Road warning lights operate at 230 V AC or 12 V DC (LED type). LED warning lights: inspect for cracked lenses and failed LEDs monthly. The flash rate must be between 55 and 65 flashes per minute. Audible warning (bell or electronic tone) must produce minimum 85 dB at 5 metres in front of the equipment housing. Measure with a calibrated sound level meter at each annual inspection. If audible warning is below 85 dB, investigate speaker unit, horn driver, or wiring before next traffic day.

## 6 Control Logic and Relay Panel
The crossing control logic is implemented in a relay or electronic interlocking panel in the crossing equipment room. Key relays are the approach detection relay (ADR), barrier control relay (BCR), and road clear relay (RCR). Test the full crossing sequence every 30 days using the authorised test procedure. Step 1: confirm road traffic is clear. Step 2: activate test mode on the crossing controller. Step 3: observe road lights start flashing, audible warning activates, barriers lower. Step 4: simulate train passing (reset detection). Step 5: observe barriers raise fully, road lights stop, audible warning stops. The road clear relay must not close (barriers must not rise) until at least 10 seconds after the tail of the train has passed the exit track circuit or axle counter. Relay contacts must be cleaned with appropriate contact cleaner every 12 months. Relay contact resistance must be below 0.1 Ω; replace relay if above 0.1 Ω after cleaning. Test all relay hold-down bolts for tightness at each inspection; vibration can loosen relays and cause intermittent failures.

## 7 Power Supply and Battery Backup
The crossing operates from 230 V AC mains supply. A battery backup (VRLA 12 V, 100 Ah) must maintain full crossing operation for a minimum of 8 hours during mains power failure. Test battery backup by disconnecting mains and operating the crossing for 30 minutes every 90 days. Verify battery voltage remains above 11.5 V at the end of the test. Battery float charge voltage must be 13.5 to 13.8 V. Measure battery internal resistance every 12 months; resistance above 20 mΩ per 100 Ah indicates battery replacement is required. Replace VRLA batteries every 5 years regardless of condition. The battery charger output must be current-limited to 10 A maximum during recharge from a deep discharge. Mains supply earth-fault protection must trip within 0.4 seconds for fault current above 30 mA; test the RCD (Residual Current Device) using the test button monthly.

## 8 Fault Diagnosis
Barriers fail to lower when train approaches: check approach track circuit or axle counter operation, check ADR energisation, check barrier motor power supply and control relay. Barriers stuck in down position: check RCR energisation, check exit track circuit or axle counter for residual detection (possible broken rail bond holding the detection circuit), check timer relay operation. Barrier motor runs but barrier does not move: check clutch engagement, inspect gearbox for seized bearings. Road lights not flashing: check 230 V AC or 12 V DC supply to lamp circuits, check flash relay or electronic flasher unit, inspect lamp wiring for damage. Audible warning not operating: check solenoid or electronic tone generator fuse, check wiring continuity, measure supply voltage at the warning device terminals. False activations (crossing operates with no train present): check for track circuit interference from stray traction currents, check axle counter sensor air gap, inspect wiring for earth faults. Barrier arm oscillating after lowering: check motor brake adjustment, check counterweight balance. Barriers lower too slowly: measure motor current and voltage, check for mechanical binding.

## 9 Emergency Procedures
If barriers fail to lower when a train is approaching: the crossing keeper or maintenance technician must immediately contact the nearest signal box and the train controller to stop approaching trains. Place a red flag or detonators on both approach sides of the crossing to protect it. Do not attempt to manually lower barriers by pushing; they are heavy and the motor brake may be locked. If barriers are stuck in the down position: check if any road traffic is trapped between the barriers; if so, do not raise barriers until the trapped vehicle can clear safely. Contact the signal box to arrange train stoppage if a vehicle is unable to clear quickly. At manually-operated crossings, the keeper must not open the gates until the train has passed and the line is confirmed clear by the advanced signal returning to danger. All level crossing failures must be reported to the District Signal Engineer within 2 hours on Form ST-7 (Crossing Failure Report).

## 10 Scheduled Maintenance Intervals
Daily (keeper-operated crossings): Lubricate gate pintles, test warning lights and bell, inspect gate locking mechanism. Weekly: Inspect barrier arm for damage, clean lamp lenses, check battery float charge voltage. Monthly: Full functional test of approach detection, barriers, road lights, and audible warning. Inspect rail bonds and test resistance. Clean relay contacts. Check barrier arm counterweight balance. Quarterly: Lubricate pivot bearing and gearbox inspection. Test battery backup for 30 minutes. Measure audible warning level. Test road light flash rate. Check clutch breakaway torque. Annual: Full relay panel inspection and cleaning. Replace VRLA battery if required. Megger test all external wiring. Repaint barrier arm banding. Test all fuses and MCBs. Calibrate approach detection distance. Measure all contact resistances.
