# Sri Lanka Railways — Signal Equipment Maintenance Manual
# Applies to: Color Light Signals, Semaphore Signals, Point Machines, Level Crossings
# Document Reference: SLR-MM-SG-004 | Revision 5

## 1 Signal Types and Overview
Sri Lanka Railways operates a mixture of signalling systems. The mainline between Colombo Fort and Kandy uses CTC (Centralised Traffic Control) with colour light signals. Branch lines use tokenless block or electric staff working with semaphore signals. Station areas use interlocked colour light signals controlled by relay-based interlocking or electronic interlocking. Colour light signals display red (stop), yellow (caution), and green (proceed) aspects using LED or filament lamp assemblies. Response time from aspect command to aspect display must not exceed 300 milliseconds. Signal aspects must be visible at a minimum distance of 400 metres in daylight and 800 metres at night.

## 2 Point Motor Maintenance
Electric point machines (also called point motors or switch machines) move the switch rails to route trains. The most common type on Sri Lanka Railways is the HW-type motor operating at 110V DC. Point machines must be inspected every 30 days. Check the following at each inspection: motor current during operation (normal 3-5 amps), operation time to complete a throw (normal 3-4 seconds), condition of slide chairs (the wear plates on which the switch rail slides), locking device engagement, detection contacts, and all wiring connections. If motor current exceeds 7 amps, investigate before the next service. High current indicates obstruction in the switch rail or worn slide chair plates. Slide chairs must be lubricated with a thin film of point lubricant every 15 days; use only approved point lubricant to avoid contamination. Point machines must be cleaned of leaves, grit, and debris during each inspection. A stalled point motor must be examined for foreign objects, binding switch rails, and worn driving components before resetting.

## 3 Response Time Standards
Signal relay response time (from energisation to aspect change) must not exceed 300 milliseconds. Measure response time using a calibrated signal timing meter. Response time exceeding 300 milliseconds requires investigation. Common causes include corroded relay contacts, damaged signal cable, loose terminal connections, or failing lamp driver circuit. Clean relay contacts with appropriate contact cleaner. Measure contact resistance with a low-resistance meter; resistance above 0.5 ohm requires relay replacement. Replace any relay with burnt or pitted contacts. Inspect signal cable for damage, moisture ingress, or rodent damage. Retighten all terminal connections and measure cable loop resistance. Voltage output at the signal head must be within 2 percent of nominal (24V for LED signals). LED signal units with response time above 300 milliseconds after all wiring is verified serviceable must be replaced as a unit.

## 4 Fault Diagnosis
Signal shows wrong aspect: relay wiring fault, relay contact failure, or lamp failure. Use signal test equipment to isolate the circuit. Signal not responding to commands: check track circuit detection, interlocking input wiring, and relay energisation. Intermittent aspect changes: relay vibration or contacts bouncing due to worn springs; replace relay. Signal lamp failure: replace the lamp immediately. LED signal heads have no user-serviceable lamps; replace the entire LED module. Signal response time greater than 300 milliseconds: see Section 3. Point machine does not throw: check power supply, motor condition, and slide chair binding. Point machine throws but fails to lock: adjust the stretcher bar length, check detection contacts for corrosion. Track circuit failure prevents signal from clearing: check rail bonds, fishplate electrical connections, and insulated joints.

## 5 Emergency Override Procedures
If a signal fails at danger (stuck at red), the signaller must be notified immediately. The signaller may issue a written authority for a train to pass the signal at danger only after verifying the route is clear by telephone. Do not attempt to mechanically force a signal to show a clear aspect. If a point machine fails to throw, the points must be clipped and clamped in the required position before any train movement. Clipping procedure: insert point clip into the switch rail lock, tighten with the clip bar, attach a clamping device, and place a reminder collar on the point lever. A railway track maintenance officer must authorise and supervise all emergency clipping operations. Report all emergency operations to the District Engineer within 2 hours.

## 6 Level Crossing Maintenance
Automatic half-barrier level crossings must be tested every 30 days. Test sequence: activate crossing from approaching train direction, verify barriers lower within 7 seconds, verify lights and audible warning operate, verify barriers lift fully after train passage, and verify detection circuit resets. Barrier motor current must not exceed 4.5 amps during normal operation. High motor current indicates barrier pivot bearing wear or bent barrier arm. Proximity sensors for train detection must be cleaned monthly. Test response of proximity sensors using a steel test plate. Replace sensors with drifting voltage output (greater than 0.5V drift from nominal). Level crossing gates must be repainted annually and all pivot bearings lubricated every 30 days. Counterweights must be balanced so the barrier arm rises without motor power on power failure.
