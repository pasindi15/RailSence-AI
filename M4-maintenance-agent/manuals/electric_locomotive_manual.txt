# Sri Lanka Railways — Electric Locomotive Maintenance Manual
# Applies to: Class EL-3000 and EL-4000 Electric Locomotives, Bo-Bo and Co-Co wheel arrangements
# Document Reference: SLR-MM-EL-006 | Revision 2

## 1 Overview
The EL-3000 and EL-4000 class electric locomotives operate on 25 kV AC overhead electrification on Sri Lanka Railways electrified sections. The EL-3000 produces 3,000 kW continuous tractive effort; the EL-4000 produces 4,000 kW. Both classes use a pantograph to collect current from the overhead contact wire, step it down through a traction transformer, convert it via an IGBT-based inverter to three-phase AC, and drive AC induction traction motors, one per axle. Regenerative braking feeds energy back to the overhead supply during deceleration. The locomotive controller communicates with all subsystems via a CAN bus network. All maintenance must be performed with the overhead line de-energised and earthed unless stated otherwise. High-voltage safety regulations must be followed at all times.

## 2 Pantograph System
The pantograph collects current from the 25 kV overhead contact wire. The collector strip (carbon or metallic-impregnated graphite) slides against the contact wire and must be checked at every depot visit. Minimum acceptable collector strip thickness is 10 mm; replace when worn to 12 mm to allow for variation. Misaligned contact wire tracking causes rapid asymmetric strip wear; investigate contact wire stagger if strip shows uneven wear across its width. Pantograph contact force must be within 70 to 110 N. Measure using a contact force gauge at the pantograph test rig. Contact force below 70 N risks dewirement; above 110 N causes excessive overhead wire wear. Adjust the pneumatic lifting pressure or spring pre-load according to the manufacturer's calibration table. Inspect the pantograph frame for cracks using dye penetrant every 90 days. Inspect the raising and lowering mechanism for binding or worn pivot bushes every 30 days. Lubricate all pivot pins with MoS2-based grease every 30 days. The pantograph articulation (knuckle joints) must move freely; stiff or seized joints cause poor contact wire tracking at speed. Test pantograph operation at least 5 full raise-lower cycles at each inspection. Response time from raise command to full contact must be under 7 seconds.

## 3 Traction Transformer
The traction transformer steps 25 kV AC from the pantograph down to secondary voltages for the traction inverter and auxiliary supplies. Operating oil temperature must remain below 90 degrees Celsius. Oil temperature above 100 degrees Celsius triggers an alarm; above 115 degrees Celsius, the locomotive protection system trips the main circuit breaker. Check oil level via the sight glass at every depot visit. Top up with Shell Diala S4 ZX-I insulating oil or approved equivalent. Oil samples must be taken every 6 months and tested for dielectric strength (minimum 50 kV/mm), moisture content (maximum 10 ppm), and dissolved gas analysis. An increase in combustible gas (hydrogen, methane, acetylene) in the oil indicates internal arcing or overheating; withdraw the locomotive from service and send the transformer for workshop inspection. Inspect all external bushings for cracks, tracking, and corona discharge every 30 days. Clean bushings with a dry lint-free cloth; contamination increases leakage current and causes flashover in wet conditions.

## 4 IGBT Inverter and Converter
The main traction inverter converts the transformer secondary AC to variable-voltage variable-frequency three-phase AC for the traction motors. The inverter uses IGBT (Insulated Gate Bipolar Transistor) modules rated at 3,300 V and 1,200 A per switch. Inspect all IGBT module heatsinks for dust accumulation every 30 days; clean with filtered dry compressed air. Dust accumulation increases thermal resistance and causes module overtemperature protection trips. IGBT junction temperature must not exceed 125 degrees Celsius; measure via the cooling water outlet temperature and the inverter thermal model. DC link capacitors are the highest-failure-rate component; inspect for swollen housings and measure capacitance every 12 months. Capacitors with capacitance below 90 percent of nominal must be replaced as a matched bank. Check DC link voltage ripple under load; ripple greater than 3 percent of DC link voltage indicates degraded capacitors. The inverter gate drive boards are tested by running the PWM self-test routine from the diagnostic terminal. All gate drive channels must respond within specification; a failed gate drive board causes one phase to be lost and motor unbalanced current. Cooling water flow through the IGBT heatsink cold-plates must be maintained at 15 to 20 litres per minute; check flow rate at every 30 day service.

## 5 Traction Motors
AC induction traction motors are mounted in each bogie, one per axle. Nominal power per motor: EL-3000 class 750 kW, EL-4000 class 1,000 kW. Motor winding insulation resistance must be above 10 MΩ when measured at 1,000 V DC with a megohmmeter, measured between winding and frame. Insulation resistance below 1 MΩ indicates moisture ingress or winding degradation; dry out in oven at 80 degrees Celsius for 24 hours and retest before returning to service. Motor bearing temperature is measured by PT100 sensors; maximum bearing temperature is 90 degrees Celsius under full load. Bearing temperature above 100 degrees Celsius indicates grease degradation or bearing damage; withdraw from service. Re-grease motor bearings with Mobil Polyrex EM grease every 50,000 km or 12 months. Motor forced-air cooling fans must be tested at every service; a stalled cooling fan causes rapid motor overtemperature on long grades. Motor air filters must be cleaned every 15 days in dusty environments; replace paper elements every 6 months. Check all power cable connections to the motor terminal box for tightness and signs of overheating (discolouration, burnt insulation) every 30 days; loose connections cause arcing and terminal failure.

## 6 Auxiliary Systems
The auxiliary converter supplies 415 V AC three-phase for hotel power (lighting, air conditioning, passenger power) and compressor drives. The battery charger maintains the 72 V or 110 V control battery. Control battery voltage must remain above 90 V (110 V system) or 62 V (72 V system) under all operating conditions. Measure battery voltage under load (with compressor running) at each inspection. Cell voltage below 1.9 V on any cell in a 36-cell lead-acid battery indicates a weak cell; replace the full battery bank. The motor-alternator for hotel power must be inspected every 30 days; check brush condition (minimum 15 mm), commutator surface condition (smooth, no grooving), and slip ring condition. Compressed air is supplied by a screw or reciprocating compressor; check oil level in oil-injected screw compressors every 15 days, change oil every 500 hours of run time. Air dryer moisture content must be verified by the dew point meter on the compressed air system; dew point above 3 degrees Celsius at 7 bar indicates a failing dryer.

## 7 Fault Diagnosis
Pantograph fails to rise: check compressed air supply to pantograph cylinder (minimum 6 bar), inspect the lowering valve solenoid, verify ground wire (earth brush) connection. Pantograph dewirement: inspect collector strip for uneven wear, measure contact force, inspect overhead line geometry at the incident location. Motor overtemperature trip: check cooling fan, air filter restriction, and duty cycle. Was the locomotive working a long steep grade? Reduce power or investigate ventilation. IGBT overcurrent trip: check for motor winding short (measure insulation resistance), check for mechanical blockage (bearing seizure causing locked rotor), check for traction control software fault. DC link undervoltage: check main circuit breaker closing, transformer secondary voltage, and incoming pantograph voltage during the failure. Main circuit breaker (MCB) trips repeatedly: indicates a sustained overcurrent; isolate the affected inverter channel using the isolation switch and run at reduced power. Auxiliary power failure: check the auxiliary converter output fuse, check the hotel power wiring for shorts. Control system communication error: CAN bus fault; check CAN bus termination resistors (must be 120 Ω at each end) and all node connectors for corrosion.

## 8 High-Voltage Safety Procedures
Before working on any high-voltage equipment: obtain a written Permit to Work from the Electrical Safety Officer, isolate the pantograph and confirm it is lowered using the visual indicator, earth the overhead line using the approved earthing device, lock the main isolation switch in the open position with a padlock and retain the key, apply Red Warning Labels to all isolation points, and test for absence of voltage using an approved voltage detector. Never work on inverter equipment within 5 minutes of isolation; DC link capacitors retain lethal voltage after isolation. Test DC link voltage at the test points inside the inverter cabinet with an approved voltmeter before touching any components. Treat any unknown component as live until proven dead. All personnel working near high-voltage equipment must wear IEC Class 1 rubber gloves, safety glasses, and anti-static overalls. Report any electric shock incident immediately and ensure medical evaluation is performed.

## 9 Scheduled Maintenance Intervals
Daily: Check pantograph collector strip visually, confirm pantograph raises and lowers, check cooling water level and temperature indicators, inspect for oil or fluid leaks, confirm no fault codes on driver's display. Weekly: Clean pantograph frame, check all high-voltage cable connectors for signs of overheating, check compressed air dryer dew point, inspect traction motor air filters. Monthly: Full pantograph inspection and contact force measurement, transformer oil level and temperature, clean IGBT heatsink fins, check DC link capacitor visual condition, re-grease pantograph pivot joints, battery capacity test. Quarterly: Insulation resistance test on all traction motors, transformer oil sample and analysis, replace motor air filter elements, inspect high-voltage cable insulation for cracking or chafing. Annual: Major inspection; replace pantograph collector strips, overhaul main circuit breaker, replace DC link capacitors if below specification, overhaul traction motor bearings, full CAN bus network diagnostic scan, all contactors and relays tested and replaced if worn.

## 10 Mileage-Based Service Intervals
Pantograph collector strip replacement: every 20,000 km or when worn to 12 mm. Pantograph pivot bush replacement: every 100,000 km. Transformer oil sample: every 50,000 km or 6 months. IGBT module replacement: on-condition (replace when test fails) or preventively at 500,000 km. DC link capacitor bank replacement: every 300,000 km or 8 years. Traction motor bearing re-grease: every 50,000 km or 12 months. Traction motor winding insulation test: every 30,000 km or 6 months. Main circuit breaker contact replacement: every 200 operations or 5 years. Battery bank replacement: every 5 years or when any cell drops below specification. Screw compressor oil change: every 500 running hours. Air dryer desiccant replacement: every 3 years or when dew point rises above specification.
