# Sri Lanka Railways — Diesel Engine Maintenance Manual
# Applies to: DE Series (Class DE-1000 and DE-2000), B-B wheel arrangement
# Document Reference: SLR-MM-DE-001 | Revision 4

## 1 Overview
The DE Series diesel engines power the majority of long-distance and intercity services on Sri Lanka Railways mainline routes. The DE-1000 class produces 1,000 HP at the rail and the DE-2000 class produces 1,500 HP. Both classes use a direct-injection turbocharged diesel engine coupled to an alternator powering four DC traction motors, one per axle. Scheduled maintenance intervals are defined in this manual and must be adhered to by all depot maintenance teams. Any deviation requires written authorisation from the Chief Mechanical Engineer.

## 2 Engine Components
The core engine assembly consists of the crankcase, cylinder block, cylinder head, pistons, connecting rods, crankshaft, and camshaft. The fuel system includes an injection pump, fuel filters, injectors, and a lift pump. The cooling system comprises a radiator, water pump, thermostat, expansion tank, and fan. The lubrication system uses a gear-type oil pump, full-flow oil filter, oil cooler, and sump. The turbocharger forces air into the intake manifold at up to 1.8 bar boost pressure. All gasket seals must be replaced during major overhaul. Crankcase breather pipes must remain unobstructed to prevent oil pressure build-up.

## 3 Oil System Maintenance
Engine oil must be changed every 30 days or 15,000 km, whichever comes first. Use only SAE 15W-40 heavy duty diesel oil conforming to API CI-4 specification. Oil pressure at normal operating temperature must be between 3.8 and 5.2 bar. If oil pressure falls below 3.5 bar, remove the locomotive from service and investigate immediately. Common causes of low oil pressure include worn oil pump gears, blocked oil strainer, excessive bearing clearance, or low oil level. Oil leaks are most commonly found at the crankshaft rear seal, rocker cover gasket, oil pan gasket, and around the oil filter housing. Any oil seepage around the main gasket seal must be pressure tested. If an oil leak is confirmed, tighten all relevant fasteners and replace gaskets as required. A cracked oil pan requires immediate replacement. Do not operate an engine with oil level below the minimum mark on the dipstick.

## 4 Cooling System
Normal operating coolant temperature is 78 to 88 degrees Celsius. An engine temperature exceeding 100 degrees Celsius is an overheating warning. Shut down the locomotive if temperature reaches 105 degrees Celsius. Investigate immediately. Check coolant level in the expansion tank first. Low coolant level indicates a leak in the system. Inspect hoses, hose clips, radiator, water pump seal, and cylinder head gasket for leaks. Flush and replace coolant every 12 months using a 50 percent water and 50 percent ethylene glycol mixture. Clean radiator fins with compressed air every 15 days. A blocked thermostat prevents the engine from reaching operating temperature and must be replaced. Thermostat opens at 82 degrees Celsius and is fully open at 95 degrees Celsius.

## 5 Fuel System
Fuel injection pressure must be set between 200 and 240 bar. Fuel delivery pressure below 180 bar indicates a failing injection pump or blocked fuel filter. Replace the primary and secondary fuel filters every 30 days. Vent the system after filter replacement by operating the manual priming pump. Fuel injectors must be tested and reconditioned every 180 days. Signs of a faulty injector include black exhaust smoke, loss of power, and engine misfire. Fuel tanks must be drained and cleaned annually. Water contamination in fuel causes injector corrosion and must be removed immediately by draining water from the tank sediment bowls daily.

## 6 Fault Diagnosis
If the engine fails to start, check battery voltage first (minimum 24V for cranking), then verify fuel supply, and inspect the starter motor. Starter motor failure is indicated by no cranking or very slow cranking. Check starter motor brushes for wear; replace when worn below 10mm. Overheating with coolant loss indicates a blown head gasket or cracked cylinder head. Confirm with a combustion gas test on the expansion tank. Blue smoke on startup indicates oil burning, usually from worn piston rings or valve stem seals. Black smoke at load indicates fuel system fault, often blocked air filter or failing injector. Engine vibration beyond normal limits indicates broken engine mount or internal component failure. Shut down immediately and investigate.

## 7 Scheduled Maintenance Intervals
Daily: Check engine oil level, coolant level, fuel level, and battery voltage. Inspect for leaks. Weekly: Check fan belt tension, clean air filter pre-cleaner, inspect fuel tank sediment bowls. Monthly: Change engine oil and filter, replace fuel filters, inspect hoses and belts, test coolant concentration. Quarterly: Overhaul fuel injectors, test thermostat, inspect radiator, check all gasket seals. Annual: Major overhaul including crankcase inspection, replace timing chain or belt, recondition cylinder head, replace all cooling system hoses, flush fuel tank. Health score below 50 indicates overdue maintenance. Score below 30 indicates critical maintenance required and service withdrawal.

## 8 Mileage-Based Service Intervals
Mileage and kilometre repair schedule for DE-1000 and DE-2000 class locomotives. Use these km intervals to plan when to repair, service, or overhaul engine components. Where both a time and mileage interval are listed, whichever is reached first triggers the service. Average operating distance for Sri Lanka Railways mainline locomotives is approximately 400 to 600 km per day. Engine oil and oil filter change: every 15,000 km or 30 days. Primary and secondary fuel filter replacement: every 15,000 km or 30 days. Air filter element replacement: every 7,500 km or 15 days. Fan belt inspection and tension check: every 5,000 km or weekly. Fuel injector reconditioning: every 50,000 km or 6 months. Coolant flush and replacement: every 60,000 km or 12 months. Radiator fin cleaning: every 3,000 km or 15 days. Turbocharger inspection and bearing check: every 100,000 km or 12 months. Cylinder head top overhaul (valve grinding, seat recondition): every 300,000 km or 3 years. Major engine overhaul (crankshaft regrind, piston ring replacement, bearing replacement): every 600,000 km or 5 years. Timing chain or timing belt replacement: every 600,000 km or 5 years during major overhaul. Starter motor brush inspection: every 30,000 km or 6 months; replace brushes when worn below 10 mm. Engine mount inspection for cracking or displacement: every 50,000 km or 6 months.

## 9 Turbocharger Maintenance
The turbocharger forces compressed air into the engine intake at up to 1.8 bar boost pressure, significantly increasing power output. Turbocharger rotor speed typically reaches 80,000 to 120,000 RPM during full power. At these speeds, bearing condition is critical. Inspect turbocharger bearing clearance every 100,000 km or 12 months by checking shaft radial play; radial play above 0.25 mm indicates bearing wear requiring replacement. Axial play must not exceed 0.15 mm. Listen for unusual noise from the turbocharger during each startup inspection; a high-pitched whine or grinding indicates bearing failure. Inspect the compressor inlet for oil contamination every 30 days; oil in the inlet indicates a failed compressor seal allowing crankcase pressure to push oil through. Oil contamination into the intake causes carbon buildup in the intercooler and compressor wheel. Inspect the compressor wheel blades for erosion, pitting, and tip damage. A blade damaged beyond the manufacturer's repair limits requires turbocharger replacement. The intercooler (charge air cooler) must be inspected and cleaned every 90 days; blockage increases back-pressure and reduces power. Test intercooler by pressure testing at 1.5 bar with water pressure; any leak requires repair before return to service. After any turbocharger replacement, run the engine at idle for 5 minutes before applying load to prime the bearing oil supply.

## 10 Engine Starting and Battery Systems
The diesel engine starts using an electric starter motor powered by a 24 V lead-acid battery bank (typically 4 x 6 V batteries in series). Battery specific gravity must be between 1.270 and 1.290 when fully charged at 25 degrees Celsius; measure with a hydrometer at each monthly service. A fully charged battery should hold 25 to 26 V on open circuit; voltage below 24 V open circuit indicates a discharged or failing battery. Test battery capacity by load testing at the 20-hour discharge rate; any battery unable to sustain 22 V for 5 minutes under load must be replaced. Clean battery terminals and cable connections monthly; apply petroleum jelly after cleaning to prevent corrosion. The alternator charges the battery during engine operation. Alternator output voltage must be 27 to 28.5 V DC at 1,500 RPM. Alternator output below 26.5 V indicates a failing voltage regulator or worn brushes; measure brush length (minimum 8 mm) and replace if worn. The starting system includes a pre-heat system for cold weather starts; glow plugs or intake air heaters reduce white smoke on cold starts. Test glow plug resistance; a serviceable glow plug reads 0.5 to 1.5 Ω.

## 11 Exhaust System and Emissions
The exhaust system routes combustion gases from the engine through a silencer and exhaust manifold to atmosphere. Inspect the exhaust manifold for cracks and leaks every 30 days; exhaust leaks cause carbon deposits in the engine bay and are a fire risk. Replace the exhaust manifold gasket at each major overhaul or if any crack is found. Exhaust smoke colour diagnosis: white smoke on cold start is normal condensation; persistent white smoke at operating temperature indicates coolant entering combustion (blown head gasket). Blue smoke at any load indicates oil burning from worn rings or valve seals. Black smoke under load indicates rich fuel mixture, blocked air filter, or failing injector. Grey-black smoke at high load indicates turbocharger issues or air restriction. Measure exhaust backpressure at the manifold outlet; backpressure above 75 mbar at full load indicates a blocked silencer or kinked exhaust pipe. Clean or replace the silencer if blockage is confirmed.

## 12 Workshop Safety During Engine Maintenance
Never run the engine in an enclosed workshop without adequate ventilation; diesel exhaust contains carbon monoxide and particulates that are harmful to health. Use calibrated CO detectors in any workshop where engines are run indoors. Diesel fuel is flammable; no open flames or smoking within 10 metres of fuel handling. Drain fuel into approved fire-safe containers; never allow fuel to spill on hot engine surfaces. Compressed air from the turbocharger or air systems can cause injury; always depressurise before disconnecting any air connection. After replacing fuel injectors, run the engine at idle for 5 minutes and check all connections for leaks before applying load. Hot coolant under pressure causes severe burns; always allow the engine to cool and release pressure before opening the cooling system. Wear eye protection and heat-resistant gloves when working near exhaust components. Dispose of used engine oil, fuel filters, and coolant as hazardous waste.
